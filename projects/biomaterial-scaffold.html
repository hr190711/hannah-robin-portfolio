Mechanical architecture / Biomaterials

Designing a multichannel nerve-guidance scaffold

Developing the mechanical scaffold architecture for a four-person team’s concept to bridge traumatic brachial plexus nerve gaps.

My roleFour-person course team · Mechanical scaffold design
ContextMIT 3.051 · Biomaterial Device Design
WhenFall 2026 · ongoing

Three-panel CAD figure of a conceptual nerve guidance conduit with hexagonal microchannels, proximal and distal sleeves and grooved channel walls · Click image to enlarge

The problem

Traumatic brachial plexus injuries can leave nerve gaps that are difficult to repair. Our four-person team is researching a nerve-guidance conduit concept that could bridge a gap while providing a structured path for regenerating axons.

My contribution

I am primarily responsible for the mechanical scaffold architecture. I proposed the hexagonal channels and honeycomb-style internal packing, aiming to create multiple physically guided pathways within a cylindrical conduit. I also worked through how that internal structure relates to the proposed nerve fixation sleeves and the mechanical role of the scaffold.

Design concept

Our team-created CAD figure shows three views: a cutaway of the proposed conduit, a longitudinal layout with proximal and distal nerve sleeves, and a close-up of hexagonally packed microchannels with grooved surfaces. The goal of the internal geometry is to provide organized physical guidance while fitting within the broader biological and biomaterials design constraints.

Project status

This is preliminary academic design work. The conduit has not been manufactured, mechanically tested, or shown to improve nerve regeneration. The current phase is about developing a plausible architecture from literature, biological requirements, and mechanical reasoning.

Why it interests me

The project connects the parts of engineering that interest me most: mechanical design, materials, and technology intended to restore human function. I enjoy taking a biological problem and asking how geometry and structure can make a difference.
